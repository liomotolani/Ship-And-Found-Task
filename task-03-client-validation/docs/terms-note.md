# Terms note

## Where the wording came from

The terms wording on the VetReply sign-up form was **written by an AI
agent**. It was not adapted from a legal template, and it has not been
checked by anyone with legal knowledge.

## The wording on the form

> **Staff review:** VetReply drafts replies from practice-approved
> documents. Staff review and approve every reply before it is sent.
>
> **Emergency care:** VetReply is not an emergency service. Online enquiries
> are not monitored for emergencies.
>
> **Eligibility & security:** You must be 18 or over, keep your account
> secure, and use VetReply lawfully.
>
> **Transparent pricing:** Fees and membership terms are shown before
> payment, securely handled by Stripe.
>
> Read full terms and conditions (opens in a new tab)
>
> I have read and agree to the terms and conditions.

## Why this isn't acceptable for real users

VetReply collects personal data and will take payments, so users are
agreeing to terms that have legal weight. Wording generated by an agent may
sound right while leaving out things the law requires, promising things the
product doesn't do, or using terms that don't hold up if challenged. "An
agent wrote it" is not a reason to trust it.

This wording also makes promises the code doesn't keep yet. The user ticks
that they agree, but the server doesn't save that acceptance with their
account (`defects.md`, Defect 2), so there is no record they agreed.

## What I will do before real users sign up

1. Replace this wording with terms adapted from an established template for
   a UK service that handles personal data and payments.
2. Have the adapted wording checked by someone qualified.
3. Save each user's acceptance, with the date and the version of the terms
   they accepted, so there is a record if it is ever questioned.
